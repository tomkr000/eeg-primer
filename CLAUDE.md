# Project notes for Claude Code

Interactive, cited explainer of the physical limits of scalp EEG for non-invasive brain–computer interfaces (BCI).
Vite + vanilla TypeScript + D3; no framework. `npm run dev` for hot reload, `npm test` for the solver tests, `npm run build` type-checks.

## Rules that keep the site rigorous
- Every number or physical statement shown on the page must map to a claim in `src/data/claims.ts` with at least one reference in `src/data/references.ts`, cited in the section text via `claimCite('<id>')`. Never type a number into prose without a claim.
- A claim's `status` is set only from an independent check of the source (see `docs/verification.md`): `verified`, `partially` (with the nuance in `note`), `unverified`, `derived` (follows from cited inputs), or `assumption` (a modelling choice with no literature value). Do not upgrade a status without opening the source.
- Default parameter values live in `src/data/params.ts` and must agree with the claim they cite.
- Spell out abbreviations on first use in page copy (e.g., "cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)").
- Keep code concise; prefer editing an existing module over adding a dependency.

## Physics
- `src/physics/sphere.ts` solves the concentric-shell head model analytically (Legendre expansion per degree, continuity of potential and radial current). Tests in `tests/sphere.test.ts` compare with the closed-form homogeneous sphere and check interface continuity; keep them green.
- Units are SI inside the physics modules (m, S/m, A·m, V); sections convert for display.
- `src/data/leadfield-svd.json` is generated by `scripts/leadfield_svd.py` (MNE-Python, fsaverage BEM). If you regenerate it, update the numbers in claim `svd-counts` and `tests/leadfield.test.ts` together.
- `src/physics/control.ts` serves sections 7 to 9: `restVersusImagery` (rest and imagery band-power estimates gamma distributed with shape B·T, the best threshold where the densities cross), `mWayCorrect` (an m-way imagery choice read in independent channels), `bandNoise` (a unit-power band-limited Gaussian process), `snrVersusElectrodes` (the matched-filter SNR of one patch from N electrodes on the section 3 sphere: exact in electrode space up to 128 electrodes, per spherical-harmonic degree above, the two checked against each other in `tests/control.test.ts`) and `ceilingPerNam` (the noiseless, infinitely dense limit). `P.erdPowerDrop` (claim erd-depth) and `P.decoderBandHz` (claim erd-detect) are shared by sections 7, 8 and 9.
- Section 8 (`src/sections/s8-delay.ts`): `simulateTrial(seed, onset, ramp, T, drop)` runs one imagined movement through the decoder's chain with the section 5 synthesiser (`envelope` and `rhythm` options: the rhythm is `bandNoise`, so its power waxes and wanes as in section 7): the raw signal, its 8 to 13 Hz band with the expected amplitude, and the trailing-window power relative to the resting level (the resting rhythm and the noise through the same band-pass), the threshold halfway to the fully suppressed level. Outcomes: the first downward crossing within `DEADLINE` (4 s) after the intention moves the arm, a crossing in the `EARLY_S` (1.5 s) before it is a false start, none is a miss; `outcomeRates` draws `MC` random trials for the readouts (debounced after a slider moves). The figure is three stacked plots (raw, band, power) with legends below them, four sliders (window, ERD depth, onset, ramp) and the robot arm: every press draws a new trial (seed++), moves at once on a false start, and shows the outcome and the rates on the canvas. Timing defaults (`P.erdOnsetS`, `P.erdRampS`) follow claim `erd-timecourse`.
- `src/data/head-leadfield.json` (the head view of section 4) is generated by `scripts/export_head_leadfield.py`: ico-4 sources (about 4 mm apart; ico-3 left a 10 mm patch with only 4 to 7 points and mirror patches up to twice as strong as each other) and BioSemi-128 electrodes in head coordinates (mm) plus the lead field in µV per nA·m as base64 int16 with one float32 scale per source.

## Layout and copy
- Every section is a title, an "In depth" toggle holding the two-column prose (collapsed by default), then one figure sized to a laptop screen (visuals left, sliders right, readouts under the visuals, caption below). Keep figure heights under about 650 px at 1366 × 768; Playwright screenshots are the check.
- Sliders show the title (16 px, bold) above the value (13 px); carets under the track mark reference values (`marks` option), e.g. the typical patch and "Seizure" at full synchrony. Sections 2 to 7 share one source model: N cells and the fraction in step (`P.patchDefaultN`, `P.patchDefaultSync`, `P.patchMaxN`), never a bare moment slider; sections 4 to 7 take the pair from `sourceSliders()` in `s5-noise.ts`, and `alphaUv(N, f)` there is the alpha amplitude at the scalp through the section 3 head.
- Section 4 uses `src/sections/dim-model.ts`: the fsaverage lead field, patch patterns and one `state` (patches, N, fraction in step, radius) with `subscribe`/`notify`; `dimControls()` builds the N, fraction and radius sliders bound to that state. The patch set is fixed (the motor areas, a representative set, numbered without names): no presets, no clicking, no reset. Section 4 (`s4-blur.ts`, id `blur`) is three titled head views in a row (`head-view.ts`: locations of patches, each patch alone, the sum of all patches on an absolute log colour scale; no noise, which arrives in section 5) and uses `figure(..., { wide: true })`.
- Section 7 (`s7-dimensions.ts`, id `dimensions`) is the measured response matrix: the mean change of mu power at C3, Cz and C4 during four imagined movements (claim mi-matrix, Pfurtscheller et al. 2006 Table 1, group means and SDs in `MOVES`), drawn as a dot plot (twelve rows, the band of one window's scatter from the gamma model with shape B·T, the mean as a tick, one random draw as a dot) beside the section 4 head with the three electrodes as discs shaded by the same draw. Controls: a `choice` of imagined movement, the window, the shared N and synchrony sliders (which set the noise floor 1/ρ through section 9's 64-electrode channel), and a "Draw another window" button (seed++). Readouts: Monte Carlo decoding of one window from the three electrodes (`classifyWindows`, the gamma likelihood), left-or-right, feet-or-tongue and four-way with Wolpaw bits/s, and the record of 3. The figure is `wide`. The older models (a patch-counting SNR ladder, then a rest-versus-imagery density) are in git history; the independent challenge of the first is `docs/research/section7-challenge.md`.
- Section 9 (`s9-count.ts`, id `count`) plots the patch SNR against electrode count (log axes, 2 to 100,000) with electrode noise alone and with the brain's background, the ceiling, and the imagery accuracy for the same counts with its cap and a band where measured decoders peak; one slider per row so the electrode-count marks have room.
- No em dashes in page copy, captions, labels or docs (real paper titles excepted). Use a colon, a comma or a new sentence instead.
- Never name a colour in copy, captions, legends or canvas notes: show it. In HTML use `sw(color, kind)` from `src/ui/layout.ts` (patch, line, dash, thick, glow); on a canvas use `textWithSwatches` from `src/ui/field.ts` with `{ sw: colour }` parts.
- At most five sliders per figure. A slider earns its place only if moving it changes the figure's message; anything else is fixed at its cited value and listed, with its citation, in the figure's assumptions table. Slider ranges stay inside the cited measured range (plus a zero or extreme end only when it makes a point).
- The detection floor used across sections 2, 3 and 4, and the default electrode noise of sections 7 and 9, is 1 µV, the lower end of measured electrode noise (claim skin-noise); section 1 uses a fixed 5 µV microelectrode floor (claim spike-noise-floor) and draws no probe.
- `section(root, id, number, title, subtitle?)` takes an optional one-line subtitle. The unnumbered aside after section 4 (`src/sections/synchrony.ts`, id `synchrony`) is titled “From patches to EEG signals”. Its introduction connects section 4’s spatial snapshot to the voltage recorded over time at one electrode; its closing link leads to section 5’s alpha-plus-noise recording. It reuses the section 4 head geometry with schematic signal regions, short HTML callouts that name the action and resulting signal change, and five brief notes. A compact table distinguishes the user action from the measured EEG change for movement rhythms, P300, flicker responses and slow cortical potentials. The slow-potential explanation connects feedback training to repeated choices between groups of letters. Three illustrative traces show common input, shared pauses and travelling activity, with no numerical scale or claim to be measured data. Keep eye and muscle interference distinct from brain signals, avoid treating a scalp-coherence distance as a hard resolution limit, and do not imply that an averaged readiness potential predicts an individual intention. Sections 7 to 9 model mu at section 5's alpha strength and say so as an optimistic assumption (claim mu-vs-alpha).
- Every graph has a title above it (`vizTitle(text)` from `src/ui/layout.ts`, or the `viz-title` of a titled canvas): a plain statement of what is plotted (quantity, condition, view), never a clever phrase; legends stay in the open (`glowLegend`); the long figure description (the `figure()` caption, with its citations) is collapsed under a "Figure description" toggle, like the assumptions table. Note figures created with an empty caption and filled later keep their text in the open.
- Every caret label is a cited or clearly assumed value: cite it in the slider's `note` or in the assumptions table ("Hair gap default", "Amplifier input resistance marks" in section 6).
- Plot markers (`Marker` in `src/ui/plot.ts`) can be `callout: true` to put the label in a box at the top of the plot, level with the inside legend (section 5's eye blink). `Note`s are boxed callouts with a pointer to a data point, placed by a pixel offset or at a data position (`at`); series can be `fill`ed; `legendInside: 'right'` puts the legend top right. A strip with `highlight: true` (`src/ui/strips.ts`) gets a box around it (section 6's dry metal plate).
- Section 3 draws the sphere model unrolled (x along the scalp, y above the cortical surface) so the layers are the same flat tinted bands as sections 1 and 2 (`drawCortexLayers` with `skull` and `scalp`); the air above the scalp is kept to about 2 cm, the glow there is faded to 30% (`fadeWhere`) and the profile stops at the scalp; there is no 1 µV contour (it read as an intracranial-electrode line): instead a black bar along the scalp, with arrowheads where it runs off the picture, marks the stretch where a contact electrode sees more than 1 µV, and its length is the readout.
- Review every figure change in a screenshot before committing, cropped to the changed region and upscaled 2× (`/home/claude/shoot2.mjs` then PIL crop/resize): a styling difference that looks fine at 1366 px can vanish on the user's screen, as a grey-versus-dark contour did.
- The site is light only: no `prefers-color-scheme: dark` block (the canvases use fixed colours tuned for the light surfaces). Keep it that way.
- Fields are drawn as red/blue glows (`src/ui/field.ts`) over a numbered grid (`grid` option) so the scale is explicit; detection limits are dashed circles or a dark contour, always named in the legend under the canvas. Neuron glyphs are white with a dark halo. On-canvas text uses `CANVAS_FONT` (14 px); figure chrome (sliders, captions, axes) is 13 to 14.5 px.

## Deploy
- The site is published at https://eeg-primer.tomkr.ai by GitHub Pages from the GitHub repository's `main` branch (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`: npm ci, tests, build, upload `dist/`). Pushing to `main` is a production deploy. Keep `base: './'` in `vite.config.ts`; static files that must keep their names go in `public/`.
