# Project notes for Claude Code

Interactive, cited explainer of the physical limits of scalp EEG for non-invasive brain–computer interfaces (BCI).
Vite + vanilla TypeScript + D3; no framework. `npm run dev` for hot reload, `npm test` for the solver tests, `npm run build` type-checks.

## Rules that keep the site rigorous
- Every number or physical statement shown on the page must map to a claim in `src/data/claims.ts` with at least one reference in `src/data/references.ts`, cited in the section text via `claimCite('<id>')`. Never type a number into prose without a claim.
- A claim's `status` is set only from an independent check of the source (see `docs/verification.md`): `verified`, `partially` (with the nuance in `note`), `unverified`, `derived` (follows from cited inputs), or `assumption` (a modeling choice with no literature value). Do not upgrade a status without opening the source.
- Default parameter values live in `src/data/params.ts` and must agree with the claim they cite.
- Spell out abbreviations on first use in page copy (e.g., "cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)").
- Keep code concise; prefer editing an existing module over adding a dependency.

## Physics
- `src/physics/sphere.ts` solves the concentric-shell head model analytically (Legendre expansion per degree, continuity of potential and radial current). Tests in `tests/sphere.test.ts` compare with the closed-form homogeneous sphere and check interface continuity; keep them green.
- Units are SI inside the physics modules (m, S/m, A·m, V); sections convert for display.
- `src/data/leadfield-svd.json` is generated by `scripts/leadfield_svd.py` (MNE-Python, fsaverage BEM). If you regenerate it, update the numbers in claim `svd-counts` and `tests/leadfield.test.ts` together.
- `src/physics/control.ts` serves sections 9 and 11: `snrVersusElectrodes` (the matched-filter SNR of one patch from N electrodes on the section 3 sphere: exact in electrode space up to 128 electrodes, per spherical-harmonic degree above, the two checked against each other in `tests/control.test.ts`; section 9 takes its 64-electrode value as each patch's channel), `ceilingPerNam` (the noiseless, infinitely dense limit) and `restVersusImagery` (rest and imagery band-power estimates gamma distributed with shape B·T, the best threshold where the densities cross; section 11's accuracy). `mWayCorrect` and `bandNoise` are used by no section (`bandNoise` backs the test that simulated band power scatters as the gamma model says). `P.erdPowerDrop` (claim erd-depth) and `P.decoderBandHz` (claim erd-detect) are shared by sections 9, 10 and 11.
- Section 10 (`src/sections/s10-delay.ts`): `simulateTrial(onset, ramp, T, drop)` runs one imagined movement through the decoder's chain with the section 7 synthesizer at a fixed seed: a steady 10 Hz rhythm (no waxing and waning here, which is section 9's subject) whose power falls by `drop` along a ramp, in the section 7 noise with the skin noise at the 1 µV floor; the raw signal, its 8 to 13 Hz band (a cascade of two 2nd-order band-passes) with the expected amplitude, and the trailing-window power relative to the resting level (the resting rhythm and the noise through the same band-pass), the threshold halfway to the fully suppressed level. `tTrue` is when the true power reaches the threshold and `det` the estimate's first downward crossing after the intention; there are no random outcomes (an earlier version with false starts, misses and Monte Carlo rates is in git history). The figure is three stacked plots (raw, band, power) with legends below them, four sliders (window 0.4 to 3 s, ERD 40 to 90%, onset, ramp: the lower ends keep the estimate's jitter below the threshold margin), three readouts measuring cumulative time from intention to fade onset, expected band power at threshold, and arm onset and the robot arm, which replays the current trial on each press and moves at `det`. Timing defaults (`P.erdOnsetS`, `P.erdRampS`) follow claim `erd-timecourse`.
- `src/data/head-leadfield.json` (the head view of section 4; the current checked-in matrix has 5,124 sources) is generated by `scripts/export_head_leadfield.py`: ico-4 sources (about 4 mm apart; ico-3 left a 10 mm patch with only 4 to 7 points and mirror patches up to twice as strong as each other) and BioSemi-128 electrodes in head coordinates (mm) plus the lead field in µV per nA·m as base64 int16 with one float32 scale per source.

## Layout and copy
- Each model section is a title, an "In depth" toggle holding the two-column prose (collapsed by default), then one figure sized to a laptop screen (visuals left, sliders right, readouts under the visuals, caption below). Keep figure heights under about 650 px at 1366 × 768; Playwright screenshots are the check.
- Sliders show the title (16 px, bold) above the value (13 px); carets under the track mark reference values (`marks` option), e.g. the typical patch and "Theoretical max" at full synchrony. Sections 2 to 9 share one source model: N cells and the fraction in step (`P.patchDefaultN`, `P.patchDefaultSync`, `P.patchMaxN`), never a bare moment slider; sections 4 to 9 take the pair from `sourceSliders()` in `s07-noise.ts`, and `alphaUv(N, f)` there is the alpha amplitude at the scalp through the section 3 head.
- Section 4 uses `src/sections/dim-model.ts`: the fsaverage lead field, patch patterns and one `state` (patches, N, fraction in step, radius) with `subscribe`/`notify`; `dimControls()` builds the N, fraction and radius sliders bound to that state. The patch set is fixed (the motor areas, a representative set, numbered without names): no presets, no clicking, no reset. Section 4 (`s04-blur.ts`, id `blur`) is three titled head views in a row (`head-view.ts`: locations of patches, each patch alone, the sum of all patches on an absolute log color scale; no noise, which arrives in section 7) and uses `figure(..., { wide: true })`.
- Section 9 (`s09-dimensions.ts`, id `dimensions`) drives a robot arm of 1 to 5 joints by imagined movements (`src/physics/arm.ts`, tests in `tests/arm.test.ts`). Joint k takes movement k (right hand, left hand, feet, tongue, right shoulder). The shoulder is a model addition (claim shoulder-area) on its own sixth patch, placed from Grafton et al. 1993 (claim motor-map); it replaced an earlier contrived fifth joint that read a direction no movement reached. Every imagined movement changes all six `MOTOR()` patches by `P.miResponse` (claim mi-matrix for the hand areas and the foot area at C3, C4 and Cz; claim tongue-areas), several at once combine as in claim compound-mi; each patch is read through section 11's 64-electrode channel (noise floor 1/ρ from the shared N and synchrony sliders) as band-limited Gaussian noise over a trailing window, and a least-squares decoder calibrated on each movement alone gives one command per joint, which moves the joint beyond ±0.5 (section 10's halfway threshold). The person is an ideal controller with a noise-free forecast of their own delays, one movement at a time in the sequential mode. A bold-led line at the top of the figure (`.figure-lede`) says what the simulation shows: the person always imagines the right movement, but each one changes several patches and every reading is noisy, so untangling the overlap magnifies the noise (overlap alone is undone exactly by the decoder; do not word it as if crosstalk by itself caused the errors). Three panels in a `wide` figure: the section 4 head with the patches numbered as in section 4 (no body-part labels on the head; the caption says which is which), colored by their movement's joint and shaded by their power; the mu power each electrode sees relative to rest (`coherentPattern` patterns on 10 µV of background); the intended and decoded arms (accordion start, clipped to their halves) with each joint's decoded command since Go and a Go button that plays one attempt in real time. Controls: joints, one at a time or all at once, the window, N and synchrony. Readouts: this attempt, the share of 100 fixed-seed attempts that succeed (claim arm-success), each joint's command noise at rest, movements in use. The cap's separation of the six patches (claim cap-separates; the neighboring shoulder and hand patches separate least well) is pinned in the same test file. The older models (a patch-counting SNR ladder, a rest-versus-imagery density, the response matrix as a dot plot at three electrodes) are in git history; the independent challenge of the first is `docs/research/section7-challenge.md`.
- Section 11 (`s11-count.ts`, id `count`, title “More electrodes cannot tell two typing fingers apart”) asks whether a denser cap lets someone type with imagined finger movements: can a decoder tell neighboring fingers (index against middle) apart within one keystroke? `pairVersusElectrodes` and `pairCeilingPerNam` in `control.ts` give the SNR of one region with a second region canceled, the two discs `sep` mm apart (exact up to 128 electrodes, per degree above), and `twoWayCorrect` the exact share of correct calls between two equally likely movements (incomplete beta). Each finger's region is a 10 mm disc at mu strength, broader than the finger itself (claim finger-model, an assumption chosen so thumb against little finger lands near the measured 81%). Plots: SNR of the canceled estimate and which-finger accuracy against electrode count (log axes, 2 to 100,000), electrode noise alone and with brain background, the ceiling and the no-noise cap. Five sliders, one per row, spread down beside the plots: electrodes (default 128), the regions' distance (marks: index–middle 1.5 and 4 mm, thumb–little 16 mm, claims finger-spacing and finger-neighbors), the window (default one keystroke, 0.239 s, claim typing-speed), contact noise, brain background. Results in claim finger-count, pinned in `tests/control.test.ts`. Measured finger and channel results (claim finger-eeg) stay in the prose, not on the plots.
- Use American English in site text, comments and maintained documentation. Preserve published paper titles, author names and generated data keys.
- Keep explanations concise. Prefer short sentences, define essential terms, and distinguish measured evidence from model choices and calculated results. Put each citation beside the statement it supports.
- No em dashes in page copy, captions, labels or docs (real paper titles excepted). Use a colon, a comma or a new sentence instead.
- Never name a color in copy, captions, legends or canvas notes: show it. In HTML use `sw(color, kind)` from `src/ui/layout.ts` (patch, line, dash, thick, glow); on a canvas use `textWithSwatches` from `src/ui/field.ts` with `{ sw: color }` parts.
- At most five sliders per figure. A slider earns its place only if moving it changes the figure's message; anything else is fixed at its cited value and listed, with its citation, in the figure's assumptions table. Slider ranges stay inside the cited measured range (plus a zero or extreme end only when it makes a point).
- The detection floor used across sections 2, 3 and 4, and the default electrode noise of sections 9 and 11, is 1 µV, the lower end of measured electrode noise (claim skin-noise); section 1 uses a fixed 5 µV microelectrode floor (claim spike-noise-floor) and draws no probe.
- `section(root, id, number, title, subtitle?)` takes an optional one-line subtitle. Section 6 (`src/sections/s06-synchrony.ts`, id `synchrony`) is titled “EEG signals reveal rhythms and responses to events”. Sections 5 and 6 show their explanations inside the figure boxes and have no empty In depth toggle. It reuses the section 4 head geometry with schematic signal regions, short HTML callouts that name the action and resulting signal change, and a signal table with nothing after it (the notes on telling rhythms apart, other uses of EEG and the control caveat were removed at the user's request). A compact table explains what alpha, frontal theta, movement rhythms, P300, flicker responses and slow cortical potentials reflect and what they are useful for, including detection and research as well as control. Proposed functions of alpha and theta are explicitly qualified. Section 5’s diagram note limits a fixed map reversing sign to one fixed source and explains that mixed sources can change the map shape. The slow-potential explanation connects feedback training to repeated choices between groups of letters. Keep eye and muscle interference distinct from brain signals, avoid treating a scalp-coherence distance as a hard resolution limit, and do not imply that an averaged readiness potential predicts an individual intention. Sections 9 to 11 model mu at section 7's alpha strength and say so as an optimistic assumption (claim mu-vs-alpha).
- Every graph has a title above it (`vizTitle(text)` from `src/ui/layout.ts`, or the `viz-title` of a titled canvas): a plain statement of what is plotted (quantity, condition, view), never a clever phrase; legends stay in the open (`glowLegend`); the long figure description (the `figure()` caption, with its citations) is collapsed under a "Figure description" toggle, like the assumptions table. Note figures created with an empty caption and filled later keep their text in the open.
- Every caret label is a cited or clearly assumed value: cite it in the slider's `note` or in the assumptions table ("Hair gap default", "Amplifier input resistance marks" in section 8).
- Plot markers (`Marker` in `src/ui/plot.ts`) can be `callout: true` to put the label in a box at the top of the plot, level with the inside legend (section 7's eye blink). `Note`s are boxed callouts with a pointer to a data point, placed by a pixel offset or at a data position (`at`); series can be `fill`ed; `legendInside: 'right'` puts the legend top right. A strip with `highlight: true` (`src/ui/strips.ts`) gets a box around it (section 8's dry metal plate).
- Section 3 draws the sphere model unrolled (x along the scalp, y above the cortical surface) so the layers are the same flat bands, separated by thin lines with no tint so the glow shows through, as sections 1 and 2 (`drawCortexLayers` with `skull` and `scalp`); the air above the scalp is kept to about 2 cm, the glow there is faded to 30% (`fadeWhere`) and the profile continues into the air (claim exterior-potential), its band labeled "air: weak electrical connection"; there is no 1 µV contour (it read as an intracranial-electrode line): instead a black bar along the scalp, with arrowheads where it runs off the picture, marks the stretch where a contact electrode sees more than 1 µV, and its length is the readout.
- Review every figure change in a screenshot before committing, cropped to the changed region and upscaled 2× (`/home/claude/shoot2.mjs` then PIL crop/resize): a styling difference that looks fine at 1366 px can vanish on the user's screen, as a gray-versus-dark contour did.
- The site is light only: no `prefers-color-scheme: dark` block (the canvases use fixed colors tuned for the light surfaces). Keep it that way.
- Fields are drawn as red/blue glows (`src/ui/field.ts`) over a numbered grid (`grid` option) so the scale is explicit; detection limits are dashed circles or a dark contour, always named in the legend under the canvas. Neuron glyphs are white with a dark halo. On-canvas text uses `CANVAS_FONT` (14 px); figure chrome (sliders, captions, axes) is 13 to 14.5 px.

Section 5, ‘Local patches combine into changing scalp voltages’ (`src/sections/s05-patches.ts`, id `patches`), precedes the section 6 rhythm map. Sections 7 to 11 are the former sections 5 to 9; contents links, prose cross-references and claim section metadata follow the new numbering. Section files in `src/sections` are named `sNN-<id>.ts` in page order (zero-padded so they sort); rename them when sections move. `references.ts` holds the method, verification and reference list. Dashed broad regions contain small numbered local patches using the section 4 head drawing. Three fixed-scale traces show patches 1 and 2 contributing voltage at electrode E and their signed sum. Reinforce, Timing drifts and Cancel are schematic teaching examples, not anatomical forward-model predictions. Cancel shows equal opposite voltage contributions; the traces do not distinguish opposite source timing from opposite projection signs. It does not label opposite-phase signals independent. A separate sentence explains that source orientation can cause cancellation even with synchronized currents. There is no per-mode explanation, snapshot note or voltage-axis label under the traces; the captions distinguish internal synchrony, between-patch relationships and cancellation at a particular electrode. The general synchrony tutorial and traveling-wave discussion remain removed at the user's request.

## Prose and citation reviews
- The remaining toggled text was reviewed by first-time readers and shortened on main in November 2026. See `docs/reviews/page-prose-clarity.md`.
- Do not infer a universal degree-of-freedom or typing-speed ceiling from a competition task or the arm demo. The five assigned imagery commands, including the shoulder, are model choices. Section 10's cue timings do not measure a private decision time.
- The electrode comparison starts from section 7's shared synthetic recording, which already contains baseline recording noise. Its lower traces add the listed contact noise; they do not start from pristine scalp voltage.
- The electrode-count plots count the full model sphere. Keep empirical cap counts in prose unless a layout conversion is justified.
