# Project notes for Claude Code

Interactive, cited explainer of the physical limits of scalp EEG for non-invasive brain–computer interfaces (BCI).
Vite + vanilla TypeScript + D3; no framework. `npm run dev` for hot reload, `npm test` for the solver tests, `npm run build` type-checks.

## Rules that keep the site rigorous
- Every number or physical statement shown on the page must map to a claim in `src/data/claims.ts` with at least one reference in `src/data/references.ts`, cited in the section text via `claimCite('<id>')`. Never type a number into prose without a claim.
- A claim's `status` is set only from an independent check of the source (see `docs/verification.md`): `verified`, `partially` (with the nuance in `note`), `unverified`, `derived` (follows from cited inputs), or `assumption` (a modelling choice with no literature value). Do not upgrade a status without opening the source.
- Default parameter values live in `src/data/params.ts` and must agree with the claim they cite.
- Spell out abbreviations on first use in page copy (e.g., "cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)").
- Keep code concise; prefer editing an existing module over adding a dependency.

## Physics
- `src/physics/sphere.ts` solves the concentric-shell head model analytically (Legendre expansion per degree, continuity of potential and radial current). Tests in `tests/sphere.test.ts` compare with the closed-form homogeneous sphere and check interface continuity; keep them green.
- Units are SI inside the physics modules (m, S/m, A·m, V); sections convert for display.
- `src/data/leadfield-svd.json` is generated by `scripts/leadfield_svd.py` (MNE-Python, fsaverage BEM). If you regenerate it, update the numbers in claim `svd-counts` and `tests/leadfield.test.ts` together.
- Section 8 (`src/sections/s8-delay.ts`) simulates one imagined movement with the section 5 synthesiser (`envelope` option) and a trailing-window band-power estimate; its timing defaults (`P.erdOnsetS`, `P.erdRampS`, `P.erdDepth`) follow claim `erd-timecourse`.
- `src/data/head-leadfield.json` (the head view of sections 4 and 7) is generated by `scripts/export_head_leadfield.py`: ico-3 sources and BioSemi-128 electrodes in head coordinates (mm) plus the lead field in µV per nA·m, base64 float32.

## Layout and copy
- Every section is a title, an "In depth" toggle holding the two-column prose (collapsed by default), then one figure sized to a laptop screen (visuals left, sliders right, readouts under the visuals, caption below). Keep figure heights under about 650 px at 1366 × 768; Playwright screenshots are the check.
- Sliders show the title (16 px, bold) above the value (13 px); carets under the track mark reference values (`marks` option), e.g. the typical patch and "Seizure" at full synchrony. Sections 2 to 7 share one source model: N cells and the fraction in step (`P.patchDefaultN`, `P.patchDefaultSync`, `P.patchMaxN`), never a bare moment slider; sections 4 to 7 take the pair from `sourceSliders()` in `s5-noise.ts`, and `alphaUv(N, f)` there is the alpha amplitude at the scalp through the section 3 head.
- Sections 4 and 7 share `src/sections/dim-model.ts`: the fsaverage lead field, patch patterns, the greedy (column-pivoted QR) ordering, and one `state` (patches, N, fraction in step, radius, noise) with `subscribe`/`notify`; `dimControls()` builds a copy of the four sliders bound to that state, so the controls of both sections move together; the patch set is fixed (the motor areas, a representative set): no presets, no clicking, no reset. Section 4 (`s4-blur.ts`, id `blur`) is three titled head views in a row ("Locations of patches", "How each patch looks from outside", "How patches look together", the last on an absolute log colour scale 0.1 to 50 µV with a colour bar and with one fixed-seed draw of the contact noise added to every electrode, so the noise slider visibly changes it) and uses `figure(..., { wide: true })`: visuals across the full width, controls in a row below. Section 7 (`s7-dimensions.ts`, id `dimensions`) is the bar chart with callouts drawn on the canvas (two rows above the bars, vertical pointers) and the verdict readouts beside it; the singular-value criterion is kept for the ceiling and agrees with the greedy count to within one on the presets. Messages live on the canvases, not in captions; the noise slider starts at the quietest measured contact (1 µV).
- No em dashes in page copy, captions, labels or docs (real paper titles excepted). Use a colon, a comma or a new sentence instead.
- Never name a colour in copy, captions, legends or canvas notes: show it. In HTML use `sw(color, kind)` from `src/ui/layout.ts` (patch, line, dash, thick, glow); on a canvas use `textWithSwatches` from `src/ui/field.ts` with `{ sw: colour }` parts.
- At most five sliders per figure. A slider earns its place only if moving it changes the figure's message; anything else is fixed at its cited value and listed, with its citation, in the figure's assumptions table. Slider ranges stay inside the cited measured range (plus a zero or extreme end only when it makes a point).
- The detection floor used across sections 2, 3, 4 and 7 is 1 µV, the lower end of measured electrode noise (claim skin-noise); section 1 uses a fixed 5 µV microelectrode floor (claim spike-noise-floor) and draws no probe.
- `section(root, id, number, title, subtitle?)` takes an optional one-line subtitle (`.section-subtitle`); a question with its own toggle ("How do neurons synchronize?", `src/sections/synchrony.ts`, an unnumbered aside mounted after section 4) is `inDepth(content, 'How do neurons synchronize?')`: the question is the toggle's own text, at heading size.
- Every graph has a title above it (`vizTitle(text)` from `src/ui/layout.ts`, or the `viz-title` of a titled canvas): a plain statement of what is plotted (quantity, condition, view), never a clever phrase; legends stay in the open (`glowLegend`); the long figure description (the `figure()` caption, with its citations) is collapsed under a "Figure description" toggle, like the assumptions table. Note figures created with an empty caption and filled later keep their text in the open.
- Every caret label is a cited or clearly assumed value: cite it in the slider's `note` or in the assumptions table ("Hair gap default", "Amplifier input resistance marks" in section 6).
- Plot markers (`Marker` in `src/ui/plot.ts`) can be `callout: true` to put the label in a box at the top of the plot, level with the inside legend (section 5's eye blink). A strip with `highlight: true` (`src/ui/strips.ts`) gets a box around it (section 6's dry metal plate).
- Section 3 draws the sphere model unrolled (x along the scalp, y above the cortical surface) so the layers are the same flat tinted bands as sections 1 and 2 (`drawCortexLayers` with `skull` and `scalp`); the air above the scalp is kept to about 2 cm, the glow there is faded to 30% (`fadeWhere`) and the profile stops at the scalp; there is no 1 µV contour (it read as an intracranial-electrode line): instead a black bar along the scalp, with arrowheads where it runs off the picture, marks the stretch where a contact electrode sees more than 1 µV, and its length is the readout.
- Review every figure change in a screenshot before committing, cropped to the changed region and upscaled 2× (`/home/claude/shoot2.mjs` then PIL crop/resize): a styling difference that looks fine at 1366 px can vanish on the user's screen, as a grey-versus-dark contour did.
- The site is light only: no `prefers-color-scheme: dark` block (the canvases use fixed colours tuned for the light surfaces). Keep it that way.
- Fields are drawn as red/blue glows (`src/ui/field.ts`) over a numbered grid (`grid` option) so the scale is explicit; detection limits are dashed circles or a dark contour, always named in the legend under the canvas. Neuron glyphs are white with a dark halo. On-canvas text uses `CANVAS_FONT` (14 px); figure chrome (sliders, captions, axes) is 13 to 14.5 px.
